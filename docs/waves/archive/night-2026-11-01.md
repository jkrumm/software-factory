# The night of 2026-11-01: the plan the night lead runs

**TLDR.** Tonight the factory stops being only a spawn queue: it gets Issues (F2) and real intake from Kuma and HyperDX,
and it ships its own backlog through them. One fresh Opus lead (`PLAN.md` Wave 6) runs three disjoint tracks from about
20:00 until morning, and nobody types into an agent. Every wave leaves a measured retro entry, so the morning answers
"what should the next night do differently" with data. The review eval is agw's: its own mother runs it after the
gateway (agw `docs/waves/PLAN.md` Waves 5–9).

## What I got wrong this afternoon

I recommended a "thin loop" (alerts straight into lead spawns) over F2's full Issue machine. Having read `f2.md` again, I
withdraw that. F2's evidence, hold and reopen logic is the part that makes autonomous work safe. A thin loop would ship
fixes nobody verifies, and we would rebuild it as F2 within a week. The better move keeps F2 whole and pulls the
**intake door** forward, right after F2 Wave 1. That way real alerts become Issues from tonight, even before leads take
them automatically.

## Design challenges, and what the plan does about them

- **Too many resumers.** `hc-resume`, the restart rule, the wall watchdog, the wake and send-back each fixed a real
  failure. Today they still collided twice: an early wake from background shells, and a resume nobody continued. The
  plan adds no new liveness mechanism; the worker contract already says "never end a turn on a background shell"
  (dotfiles `skills/wave/roles/worker.md`), and every brief repeats it.
- **Markdown as a state machine.** Waves live in `PLAN.md` files that `wave-gate.py` reads with regexes. It refused a
  wave today because the word "merged" appeared in a step. F4 makes waves child Issues; tonight's F2 keeps that path
  open and builds nothing for it.
- **`AGENTS.md` is the spec.** It runs to about 38k characters, and every F2 wave would grow it. C0 cuts it to rules and
  pointers and moves the behaviour into `docs/design.md`, which is where it belongs.
- **Compatibility for nobody.** "For one release" aliases (`herdrStatus`, `capacity_readings`, `herdr_restarted`) protect
  rollbacks no one runs. One user and a drained queue make a rollback a decision, not a constraint (decision 6).
- **Visibility.** Every wave is a tab, and hidden workers show nowhere. A night of 15+ agents would bury the
  orchestrator. Decision 5 hides them under it.

## Models and effort

The role table decides every agent's tier and effort; nothing names a model id. C0 adds the role `deep` (Opus/high,
placed like a worker: a hidden child) so a hard wave can raise its worker without becoming a tab.

| Work | Role | Why |
|-|-|-|
| Night lead | `lead` (Opus/high) | holds the plan, reviews every diff, decides merges |
| C0, F2 W2, W3, W5, D, I, N, agw classify | `worker` (Sonnet/high) | patterned work against an existing design: F1's six waves ran on it |
| F2 W1 (lifecycle, inbox, replay) and W4 (evidence, hold, finalize) | `deep` (Opus/high) | novel state-machine logic that every later wave and every autonomous ship rests on; a wrong transition is silent |
| Review per wave | `/review` (agw), plus the lead's `/code-review high` on W1, W4 and I | agw's review recall is unmeasured (≤ 0.05 on legacy cases), so the hard waves get a second, independent read |
| W6's three real requests | Issue leads, tier and effort from classify within the role table | the gate measures exactly this choice |

This is also an experiment: the retro compares the `deep` waves with the `worker` waves (review findings, fix rounds,
wall time, share of the week), which is the first data for "when to raise a worker".

## Learning on the way

Each setup incident is also a fix request: before F2 W1 is live, a line under §Setup defects of the evidence page; once
it is live, an Issue (`factory issue new`) in the repo that owns the defect, so the factory's own backlog grows from its
own night. The morning section of the evidence page proposes concrete changes (role table, brief template, `/wave`
skill, this plan's shape) with the numbers behind each.

## Gates

Per wave: `make check`, `/review` with every finding fixed or answered plus one pass on the fix commits, green CI,
`gh pr merge --rebase` (`rd merge` flakes under load), `make deploy` + `make verify`, and the wave's live proof. The lead
stops starting work at the budget marks (decision 7) and finishes what runs.
